## Accepted product policy

The user explicitly replaces the former policy requiring an atomic expected-object guarantee before enabling any Trash action. **Standard macOS, user-directed, single-item Move to Trash is permitted after mandatory layered preflight and explicit confirmation, with an accepted residual pathname replacement race.** The race is not eliminated. This policy does not permit knowingly moving a replacement object, bypassing protected paths or treating an unknown protection boundary as acceptable.

Classification and assessment remain information: Active, Historical, Generated, Reinstallable and Unknown describe observed characteristics; Preserve, Review candidate, Insufficient evidence and Protected describe assessments. A review suggestion does not authorize a move. An ordinary unrecognized file or folder may be eligible for a user-requested move only when mandatory protection checks succeed. Verified active Preserve and Protected items remain non-overridable blocks. A warning acknowledgment cannot bypass them. No item is called “Safe to delete,” and Trash does not promise storage recovery.

The required hard boundaries are protected system locations, credentials/authentication stores, active application data, protected home roots, running executables and verified active dependencies/databases, protected descendants, dirty/untracked/potentially unmerged Git worktrees, selection-boundary escape and incomplete required checks. Descendant budget/permission/time failure blocks the parent. Symlink and path aliases require fresh validation; detected target/ancestor/evidence changes fail closed.

Active-use evidence has a bounded current-user scope. An ordinary non-executable, non-database item retains the existing policy of permitting a fresh, nonzero partial observation with explicit unknown-dependency warnings; **this does not establish complete non-use**. Executables, databases and applicable sensitive descendants require complete scoped observations; missing/zero or stale required observations block every target. Known active references always block, including discovered aliases. General process visibility is not expanded through privileges or new privacy permissions. The real sampler returned 516 listed / 325 inspected / 256 unreadable observations in each of three runs (187 unavailable owner IDs, 3 executable paths unavailable, 12 descriptor lists above the 512 cap, 54 descriptor paths unavailable), at 10.43, 5.64 and 4.94 ms. These explain the scoped limitation; they do not show that an individual target is inactive. [Sanitized sampler evidence](build/release-candidate-validation/focused/current-user-process-sample.jsonl) records counts only, without private process paths.

The single-item path is restored and disposable supported-API moves are verified below. The historical disabled-execution records in the other Cleanup documents are retained as history and do not define the newly accepted policy. Executable-specific completeness checks use executable mode/classifier facts; database-specific completeness uses recognized classifications, `.sqlite`, `.sqlite3` and `.db` extensions, and their `-wal`/`-shm`/`-journal` companions. Known observed active paths and object aliases block every item type. Extensionless databases, scripts and mapped libraries are not comprehensively identified or observed; ordinary unknown-dependency warnings are not proof of inactivity, and no global non-use guarantee is claimed.
